📘 Concept Notes

Convolution intuition

A convolution slides a small filter (kernel) across an image, computing a weighted sum at each position — this detects local patterns like edges or textures, regardless of where they appear in the image.

Pooling

Max/average pooling downsamples the feature map, reducing spatial size while keeping the strongest signals — this reduces computation and adds a degree of translation tolerance.

Why CNNs beat fully-connected networks on images

A fully-connected layer treats every pixel as an independent feature, ignoring spatial structure and requiring huge parameter counts. Convolutions share weights across positions, drastically reducing parameters while respecting image structure.

Transfer learning

Starting from a model pretrained on a large dataset (e.g., ImageNet) and fine-tuning it on your smaller dataset is almost always faster and more accurate than training from scratch.

💡 Tips & Pitfalls

Tips

  • Start from a pretrained backbone (transfer learning) unless you have a very large dataset.
  • padding='same' (or padding=1 for a 3x3 kernel) keeps spatial dimensions unchanged, simplifying design.
  • Visualize a few training images after augmentation — a broken pipeline is a common silent bug.

Common Pitfalls

  • Feeding un-normalized pixel values (0-255) directly into the network.
  • Mismatched input image size vs. what a pretrained backbone expects.
