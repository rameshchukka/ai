📘 Concept Notes

The recurrence idea

An RNN processes a sequence one timestep at a time, carrying a hidden state forward that summarizes everything seen so far — giving the network a form of memory over the sequence.

Why LSTM/GRU over vanilla RNN

Vanilla RNNs struggle to preserve information over long sequences due to vanishing gradients. LSTM/GRU add learned gates that control what to keep, forget, and output, handling long-range dependencies far better.

Where Transformers replaced RNNs

For most modern NLP, Transformers (self-attention) have surpassed RNNs because they process all timesteps in parallel and capture long-range dependencies more directly. RNNs remain strong for many smaller time-series problems.

💡 Tips & Pitfalls

Tips

  • For long sequences, prefer LSTM/GRU over a vanilla RNN.
  • Pad/truncate sequences to a consistent length per batch, and mask padding in the loss.
  • For most modern NLP tasks, a pretrained Transformer will outperform an RNN trained from scratch.

Common Pitfalls

  • Not masking padded timesteps, letting the model learn from meaningless padding.
  • Expecting a vanilla RNN to remember information from very early in a long sequence.
