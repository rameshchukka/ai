📘 Concept Notes

What is an embedding?

An embedding is a vector of numbers representing the 'meaning' of a piece of text, learned so that semantically similar text ends up close together in vector space — unlike keyword matching, which only sees exact word overlap.

Cosine similarity

Cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors, ignoring their length — it tells you how similar in *direction* (meaning) two embeddings are, from -1 (opposite) to 1 (identical).

Keyword vs semantic search

Keyword search (TF-IDF/BM25) matches exact terms and is excellent for names, codes, and precise terminology. Semantic search catches paraphrases and synonyms it never saw verbatim. Hybrid search combines both.

Evaluating search quality

'It looks right' isn't a metric. precision@k asks: of the top k results, how many are actually relevant? This requires a small labeled set of known correct answers to test against.

🧭 Beginner → Expert Roadmap

1

Beginner

  • Understands what an embedding vector represents conceptually
  • Can compute cosine similarity between two vectors
  • Builds a simple top-k search over a small document set
2

Intermediate

  • Implements a TF-IDF weighting scheme and compares it to bag-of-words
  • Combines keyword and semantic scores into a hybrid ranking
  • Evaluates search quality with precision@k against known answers
3

Expert

  • Chooses chunk size/overlap deliberately based on retrieval quality testing
  • Adds re-ranking on top of initial retrieval for better precision
  • Diagnoses retrieval failures by inspecting embedding space, not just guessing

💡 Tips & Pitfalls

Tips

  • Normalize embedding vectors before cosine similarity for consistent scoring.
  • Chunk size matters: too small loses context, too large dilutes relevance.
  • Always eyeball your top-k results manually before trusting a metric.

Common Pitfalls

  • Comparing embeddings from two different models — they don't share a vector space.
  • Forgetting to re-embed content after edits, leaving stale vectors in the index.
  • Using raw dot product on un-normalized vectors and misreading the scale.
