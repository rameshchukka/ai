Classical Machine Learning: Zero to Hero

Linear/logistic regression, trees, ensembles, k-NN, k-Means, SVMs, and PCA — the math and mental models behind each.

📘 Concept Notes

Every algorithm is a strategy for fitting f(X) to y

Supervised ML means finding a function that maps inputs to known outputs on training data AND generalizes to new data. Regression predicts a number; classification predicts a category. Every algorithm here is a different way to choose and fit that function.

Linear regression has both a closed-form and an iterative solution

The Normal Equation (XtX)^-1 Xty solves linear regression exactly in one shot using linear algebra. Gradient descent solves it iteratively and is what scales to non-linear models like neural networks — both converge to the same answer for linear problems.

Trees split on the question that most reduces impurity

Decision trees greedily pick, at each node, the yes/no question (Gini/entropy) that best separates classes. Unlimited depth lets a tree memorize training data perfectly while generalizing terribly — depth control is not optional.

Ensembles trade one strong model for many weak ones

Random forests train many trees in parallel on random data/feature subsets and average their votes (bagging, reduces variance). Gradient boosting trains trees sequentially, each fixing the previous ones' errors (often more accurate, easier to overfit).

🧭 Beginner → Expert Roadmap

1

Beginner

  • Explains the difference between memorizing and generalizing
  • Fits a linear regression and reads its slope/intercept
  • Trains a decision tree and reads its printed structure
2

Intermediate

  • Compares bagging vs boosting and explains the tradeoff
  • Uses k-NN and explains why feature scaling matters
  • Runs k-Means and recognizes the need to choose k
3

Expert

  • Explains the margin-maximization intuition behind SVMs
  • Applies PCA and interprets explained variance ratio
  • Runs a full split->standardize->train->evaluate->compare pipeline unaided

💡 Tips & Pitfalls

Tips

  • Random forests are a strong, low-maintenance default for tabular data.
  • Always standardize features before k-NN, k-Means, SVM, or PCA.
  • Use max_depth/min_samples_leaf on trees from the start, not as an afterthought.

Common Pitfalls

  • Fitting a scaler on the full dataset instead of train-only (data leakage).
  • Unlimited tree depth silently overfitting on small data.
  • Forgetting k-Means requires choosing k in advance.

🌍 Real-World Use Cases

  • Tabular business problems: churn, credit scoring, fraud detection
  • Fast, interpretable baselines before reaching for deep learning
  • Clustering/segmentation and dimensionality reduction for visualization
