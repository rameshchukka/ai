MLOps & Production Serving: Zero to Hero

Serving patterns, latency budgets, caching, monitoring, drift detection, safe rollouts, and cost management.

📘 Concept Notes

Production requires the system to be its own error handler and monitor

In a notebook you watch every run and fix bugs by hand. In production, requests happen thousands of times unattended — the system itself must handle errors gracefully and log enough to diagnose problems after the fact, since you won't be watching.

Tail latency matters more than average latency

A system with a 50ms average latency can still make 1% of users wait 2 full seconds. Tracking p95/p99 percentiles reveals the real worst-case user experience that averages hide completely.

Models degrade silently through drift, not just errors

Data drift is the input distribution shifting (new kinds of questions arriving). Model drift is the input-output relationship changing (what counts as correct shifts). Neither shows up as a logged error — only distribution monitoring catches them.

Never fully switch to a new model version blindly

Canary deployments release a new version to a small percentage of traffic first, expanding gradually as it proves healthy — limiting the blast radius if something is wrong, versus a full cutover that risks every user at once.

  • Assembles a full serving layer combining caching, monitoring, and budget guards

💡 Tips & Pitfalls

Tips

  • Choose the cheapest serving pattern (batch/real-time/streaming) that meets your actual latency need.
  • Route canary traffic by a stable hash of user/request ID, not randomly per-request.
  • Redact PII from LLM logs — rich observability and privacy are both required.

Common Pitfalls

  • Reporting only average latency and missing tail problems.
  • Assuming drift will show up as errors — it usually doesn't.
  • Fully cutting over to a new model version without a canary stage first.
